# Literature and priority checks

Initial searches and pre-release rechecks: 26 November 2026 (UTC). No matching published result was found for the specific statements reported here. A negative search is not a priority guarantee or peer review.

- **No dimension-only branching bound.** The exact question appears in [Cherkashin §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.13532v1#S6) and after Question 1.2 of the [MDS list](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.03403v1#S1). This is not the fixed-power question addressed in the earlier archive entry. New constructions in dimension three have degree n+2 for arbitrary n, with one heavy sink and n identical light sinks. Checked the author’s publication list, the [Volz et al. degree-four examples and star criterion](https://arxiv.org/html/0909.4270v2), planar degree-three work, precise question, recent preprints, and public GitHub references; no matching result surfaced. No claim of unboundedness at fixed p.
- **Gilbert–Steiner branching.** [MDS Question 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.03403v1#S1) asks for large-dimensional bounds at fixed p in (1/2,1). [Cherkashin's Theorem 2 and §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.13532v1) give a linear construction and discuss spherical-code upper bounds. [Volz et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/0909.4270v2) supply the one-source star criterion. [Xia's Proposition 1.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13338) counts branching vertices, not maximum branch degree as a function of dimension. Checked the exact problem, title, both authorship and alternate names, arXiv and GitHub. This result does not supply the optimal exponent for arbitrary data. The distinct dimension-only question is answered negatively in the companion paper above.
- **Upper bounds for tangent cylinders.** See [Conjecture 13.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.13972v2#S13) and [Glazyrin Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00251v1), whose nine-cylinder upper bound is 0.726. The certificate preprint gives the new bounds, the contact-tree dimensional reduction, and a pinned replay of earlier data. Search by both article identifiers, theorem terms and public code found no solution to the target radius; the new bounds are not a solution. The supplementary note excludes five parallel axes and eight coplanar axis directions at the target separation, extends the previous six-axis and all-coplanar exclusions, and verifies the new marked-kernel bounds using two implementations. A focused recheck of the conjecture identifier, associated authors and exact geometry did not surface a duplicate. A subsequent supplementary note treats nonparallel contact edges: the bridgeless reduction and the certified marked rectangle do not exclude all skew configurations. The full conjecture and the published upper bound are unchanged. The latest supplement gives an independently replayed eleven-parameter reduction via maximal contact graphs and independent-set recovery, and excludes specific orthogonal-anchor neighborhoods. No blanket exclusion of an orthogonal anchor is claimed.
- **Tangent cylinders.** The quantity is the maximum *common* radius for disjoint infinite cylinders tangent to one fixed unit sphere, not the number of pairwise mutually touching arbitrary-radius cylinders. Checked [Matić–Radoičić–Spomer, arXiv:2610.13972v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13972v2), [Glazyrin, arXiv:2611.00251v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00251v1), and the full pinned [cylie window archive](https://github.com/maticivan/cylie/tree/c306097711481fcaf359bcda94f7c32fbab13273). The archive gives a larger implicit baseline for nine and ten than the headline target radii, which the new note credits explicitly. Searches by exact setting, authors, and equivalent radius/tangent-line separation did not locate the new bounds. The configurations do not settle Conjecture 13.1 or identify the exact optimal radii.
- **Closest-tripartite theorem.** The exact statement is [Conjecture 20 of arXiv:2610.18636v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18636v2#S5), for every n ≥ 3, both distances and uniqueness among all nonisomorphic graphs. The source identifies the comparison graph and gives distance formulas, but does not prove uniqueness. Searches on the exact conjecture number, source identifier, author names, candidate description, and equivalent closest-spectrum or cospectrality formulations located no follow-up solution. The known rank-four and rank-five structure results do not compare the distances. Our separate spectral-rank note solves Problem 21 and supplies a reduction, not by itself the closest-graph theorem.
- **Spectral rank.** The target is [Problem 21 of arXiv:2610.18636v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18636v2#S5), not the two older conjectures disproved by that article or the neighboring Conjecture 20. Searches by identifier, title, author names and both conventions for the squared spectral distance, and searches for the trimmed-eigenvalue rank bound, did not locate a solution. Cauchy interlacing and use of the graph atlas are standard; the sharp constants, the rank implication and its applications are the claims of the preprint.
- **Graffiti.pc.** The three numbered statements are those in DeLaViña's [November 2026 version of Written on the Wall II](https://www.uhd.edu/academics/sciences/mathematics-statistics/wowii/files/wowiiaii.pdf), not the first Written on the Wall catalogue. Searches covered the exact numbers, combinations of invariants, [the AI Village index](https://gitlab.com/ai-village-agents/village/graffiti-verification/-/raw/main/README.md), and [the C5[K4] census](https://github.com/Kuberwastaken/c5-k4), which mentions #138 as a transcription gap, not as one of its results. Neighboring already-refuted statements use other graph invariants; they are not included here.
- **Bernoulli.** The target is [Milojević–Sudakov, Conjecture 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.30287v1). Earlier results at reciprocal probabilities and at [p = 2/k](https://1stproof.org/assets/docs/report.pdf#page=27) concern specific p; here the conclusion is for an entire real interval with a support cap. The authors' [October Feige paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10380v1) concerns a different inequality. No general solution is claimed.
- **A7.** The new claim concerns global optimality in the full two-site class at a specified potential and density, not discovery of the crystal structure. See the detailed literature audit inside the A7 supplement. 

- **Comparable-demand Gilbert–Steiner stars.** Checked [Volz et al. §3](https://arxiv.org/html/0909.4270v2), [Cherkashin §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.13532v1#S6), and the question in [MDS §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.03403v1#S1). The fixed-power estimates and the counterexample with arbitrarily unequal demands are different results. Searches combining comparable or equal sinks, exponential dimension, spherical coding and powers near one did not find matching bounds in the single-source star class. The supplement records the independent proof audit and the limits of the claim.

- **Cubic tour costs in the three-dimensional unit cube.** The exact target is [MDS Question 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.03403v1#S4) and the cubic-cost form of [Balogh–Clemen–Dumitrescu Conjecture 5.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.02839v3#S5). The tetrahedral lower bound was already known. [Gordeev](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22010v1) proves a large-dimension asymptotic, not the three-dimensional value. [Malykhin–Shchepin §3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.07344v2#S3.SS2) provided the open Spring prototypes and proposed the radical for their dilation; the note uses a closed top-level ring with an exact dilation certificate and proves the resulting tour bound. Focused searches of versions, follow-up papers, repos and equivalent formulations did not find the continuous 4–6 point theorem or the closed-curve argument. The general exact constant remains open. The companion note proves the fixed Spring order can attain the dilation in the supremum, uses joint tour edits to improve the all-points upper bound, and independently replays complete continuous-coordinate seven- and eight-point covers. The multiscale companion improves the upper bound to 16.40, checks all continuous nine-point chambers, and proves a common lower bound for all 48 symmetric fixed-order methods, not for the actual optimum.

- **Demand ratios in optimal transport stars.** Checked [Volz et al. §§3 and 5](https://arxiv.org/html/0909.4270v2), [Cherkashin §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.13532v1#S6), and the question in [MDS §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.03403v1#S1). Existing fixed-power packing estimates and degree-four unequal-demand examples do not specify the polylogarithmic dependence on demand ratios proved here. The matching exponent in the demand ratio remains open.

- **Cubic-tour local reordering and deletion.** Rechecked the MDS problem identifier, the Balogh–Clemen–Dumitrescu formulation, and searches for a three-dimensional cubic bound and continuous ten-point theorems. No independent matching bound or matching close-pair theorem was found. This companion improves the previous all-points 16.40 bound to 16.10 by conditional probes and jointly compatible two- and three-edge moves. It certifies quantitative nine-point stability and excludes close-pair ten-point counterexamples, not all ten-point sets or the full conjecture.

- **Circle and sphere irrigation near linear cost.** [MDS §2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.03403v1#S2.SS2) explicitly proposes studying the cost exponent as it approaches one. [Pegon](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.01414) supplies the Lagrangian background and rectifiability; [Colombo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11793) study stability under terminal-measure changes at fixed exponent. Focused searches of the original problem, diffuse spherical irrigation, angular quantization and near-linear logarithmic corrections did not find the sharp coefficient or quantitative detour result. The exact minimizing networks and origin branching remain open.

- **Cubic tour with ten points.** Rechecked equivalent cubic-cost and cube-root formulations of MDS Question 4.1 and the Balogh–Clemen–Dumitrescu conjecture. No matching ten-point continuous theorem surfaced. The exact ten-point supremum and a quantitative deficit for distinct points have checked finite certificates; the global upper bound stays 16.10. The public source regenerates and independently verifies the two forests using the preceding public nine-point data.

- **Generalized tropical caustic branches.** [MDS §5.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.03403v1#S5.SS7) asks for limiting curves and injectivity without two convexity inequalities. Checked [Shkolnikov §5.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.11137v2#S5.SS3), which discusses reversed edges, and [Kalinin–Lupercio–Shkolnikov §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21709v2#S4), which supplies prior Farey/Hata coefficients and convex duality. Search by the exact problem, equivalent formulations and public repositories did not find the ordered recovery or the geometric inverse for unlabelled completed uniformly convergent branches. The generally unlabelled bare endpoints case is not settled. The proof separates Euclidean convergence from telescoping tropical length.

- **Cubic-tour regions and projections.** Compared [MDS Question 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.03403v1#S4), [Balogh–Clemen–Dumitrescu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02839), [Gordeev](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22010) and the [Spring prototypes](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.07344v2#S3.SS2) with the two-interval projection, thin-slab and unequal-ball results. The exact three-dimensional constant remains open; this work rules out continuous families of all-cardinality counterexamples. A sharp fixed-order cost is not a lower bound for optimal tours.

- **Quantized entropy for branched transport.** [Brancolini–Wirth Theorem 2.4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.08467#page=8) gave constant-factor bounds for the excess energy in the same parallel-slice branched-transport model, not its leading coefficient. [Pegon–Petrache](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/24M1650193) treat quantization at a fixed price exponent. Searches combining these sources, near-linear scaling, entropy, information dimension and possible sharp constants did not find the formula stated here. The earlier sphere note covers uniform surface measure. No claim is made for the separate urban-planning functional or for minimizer geometry.

- **Bare Farey endpoint sets of zero length.** Checked [MDS §5.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.03403v1#S5.SS7), [Shkolnikov's reversed-edge setting](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.11137v2#S5.SS3), [Farey/Hata duality](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21709v2#S4) and [Mikhalkin–Shkolnikov's caustic construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17269). Searches by inverse recovery, secant directions and Cantor endpoint sets did not locate the fixed-endpoint pairing theorem or the zero-length family with uniqueness against every pointwise-convergent competitor. This supplement does not assume that a jump segment is present in the data. It does not prove injectivity for all bare endpoint sets.

- **Cubic tour with eleven points and dense-set bounds.** [MDS Question 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.03403v1#S4) and [Balogh–Clemen–Dumitrescu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02839) pose the all-cardinality target and give the known tetrahedral lower bound. [Gordeev, Appendix A.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22010v1) uses unweighted diameter-ball overlap of a squared-cost optimizer; the parabolic weight bound, face folding, fixed-dimension dense-set estimate, and exact eleven-point statement here are different. Focused searches for the same eleven-point theorem and for the weighted bound surfaced no match. The three-dimensional global bound and the open status of the general question are unchanged.

- **Arbitrary radial depths in near-linear irrigation.** The [earlier entropy note](branched-transport-entropy.pdf) assumes a single delivery distance for its spherical geometry. The [shape optimization paper of Pegon–Santambrogio–Xia](https://math.ucdavis.edu/~qlxia/Research/shape.pdf) varies the target at fixed exponent; [Pegon–Petrache](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/24M1650193) vary the number of sites at fixed exponent. A focused search for the uniform-ball coefficient and angular entropy with arbitrary radial depths did not locate a matching asymptotic. The new result treats a prescribed target law and does not resolve the minimizing geometry in [MDS §2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.03403v1#S2.SS2).
